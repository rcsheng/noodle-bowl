## 2. Brand Positioning

### 2.1 Positioning Statement

For news-curious adults who want a daily mental workout that's fun, not homework, Noodle Bowl is the daily brain game app that turns real news into play. Unlike NYT Games (single puzzle format) or trivia apps (passive recall), Noodle Bowl exercises multiple cognitive muscles — bluffing, estimation, scientific reasoning — across three focused games, every day.

### 2.2 Brand Personality

| Dimension | Noodle Bowl |
|-----------|-------------|
| Tone | Witty, warm, editorial. Never preachy or childish. |
| Reference point | The Atlantic meets Wait Wait Don't Tell Me |
| What it's not | Mass-market trivia. A news quiz. Gamified homework. |
| The feeling | The satisfying click of understanding something you half-knew |

### 2.3 Tagline Options (current + alternatives)

**Current:** *Stretch your noodle. Daily.*

This works well — "noodle" as brain is a mild bit of wordplay, "daily" signals the habit, and the imperative is gentle rather than pushy. It's not going to win a Cannes Lion but it's honest and memorable.

**Supporting lines (for ads, App Store, social):**
- *The daily brain game built from today's news.*
- *Three games. Five minutes. Real stories.*
- *Play the news, not just read it.*
- *Not a quiz. A workout.*

### 2.4 Visual Identity (Summary)

Pale blue-gray background · deep navy ink · brick-red accent · Fraunces italic display · JetBrains Mono labels. The editorial newspaper aesthetic is a genuine differentiator from every other daily game app, which use either stark minimalism (NYT Games) or loud gamification (Trivia Crack). Own the "smart newspaper that's also fun" lane.

## 3. Audience Personas

### Persona A — "The News-Game Crossover" (Primary)

**Name:** Maya, 31, marketing manager  
**Location:** Urban, US or UK  
**Habits:** Plays Wordle and Connections daily. Listens to NPR on commute. Reads newsletters but not a full newspaper. Has a group chat where Wordle results get shared.

**What she wants:** Something that makes her feel smart AND entertained in the same 5-minute window.  
**How she finds apps:** Group chat recommendations, App Store Top Charts, Twitter/Threads shares.  
**Noodle Bowl moment:** Gets The Lede question right because she remembered the story from a podcast. Shares the result. Her friend downloads it.

**Name:** James, 38, software engineer

### Persona C — "The Daily Ritual Builder" (Secondary)

**Name:** Sarah, 44, teacher  
**Habits:** Does the NYT Games every morning before school. Follows Wordle stats obsessively. Has a 180-day streak she's terrified of breaking.  
**What she wants:** More variety in her morning ritual without disrupting the habit itself.  
**Noodle Bowl hook:** The unified streak + streak shields directly address her fear of losing progress. Adding Noodle Bowl to her morning routine extends it without replacing Wordle.

### Persona D — "The Friend Who Gets Challenged" (Acquisition)

This persona doesn't know the app exists. They receive a challenge link from a friend. They play on web, get sucked in, install.

**Profile:** Any age 20–50, any background. Receptiveness is determined by: (a) whether they care enough about the news to find the question interesting, and (b) whether the challenge from a friend adds competitive motivation.

**Marketing implication:** The web-first challenge experience IS the marketing for this persona. It must be beautiful, fast, and fun before it asks for anything. Copy on the post-challenge screen is a first impression — it needs to do the job of an App Store listing.

## 4. The Name Question — "The Lede"

### 4.1 The Case For

"The Lede" is the journalistic term for the opening of a news article — the sentence that hooks you. "Don't bury the lede" is a phrase that educated news-readers will recognize. It signals editorial sophistication and fits the app's newspaper aesthetic perfectly. For the target audience (Personas A and B especially), it reads as a knowing wink rather than jargon. It's also genuinely distinctive — no other game is called The Lede.

### 4.2 The Case Against

The mechanic is about finishing a *headline*, not writing a *lede*. A lede is the opening paragraph; the game presents a headline with a blacked-out punchline. There's a modest semantic mismatch that the most newspaper-literate users will notice. "Finish The Headline" (the in-app section header) is clearer than the game name.

Additionally, for users outside the core audience — and especially for Persona D (the friend who gets challenged, possibly younger or less news-immersed) — "The Lede" might not land. The word is searchable mainly in journalism contexts.

### 4.3 Verdict

**Keep "The Lede" — it's good enough, and changing it now has costs.**

The name is distinctive, fits the brand, and the target audience will get it. The semantic mismatch (headline vs. lede) is minor enough that no user will complain once they've played the game — the reveal mechanic makes the name feel apt. Changing a game name mid-development disrupts code references, content, and any early user familiarity.

That said, the section header "Finish The Headline" should remain prominent in all marketing copy, because it communicates the mechanic instantly where "The Lede" requires inference.

### 4.4 Stronger Alternatives (if a rename were warranted)

These are ranked by how well they balance brand fit, mechanic clarity, and memorability:

| Name | Mechanic Fit | Brand Fit | Memorable | Notes |
|------|-------------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| **The Pitch** | ★★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | Best alternative. "Pitch" = a reporter pitching their story completion. Dual meaning (sports/media). Active, competitive feel. |
| **The Scoop** | ★★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★★★★★ | Warm, playful, distinctly journalistic. Everyone knows "got the scoop." Slightly more casual than NB's tone. |
| **The Bluff** | ★★★★★ | ★★★ | ★★★★ | Most mechanically accurate — the game IS a bluffing game. Loses the journalism aesthetic. |
| **The Hook** | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | Journalistic (a hook draws readers in) and colloquial. Slightly generic. |
| **The Desk** | ★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★★★ | "The news desk" — strong editorial feel, but mechanic isn't obvious. |
| **The Reporter** | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★ | Clear (three reporters, pick the truth-teller) but descriptive rather than evocative. |

**If forced to rename:** Go with **The Pitch**. It describes the mechanic exactly (three reporters pitching their version of the headline ending), has an active/competitive connotation, and still reads as journalistic without requiring journalism knowledge to understand.

## 6. Launch Plan

### 6.1 Pre-Launch (Now — ~8 weeks out)

**Week 1–2: Foundation**
- Set up `noodlebowl.app` landing page (game demo, waitlist signup, App Store coming soon badge)
- Create @NoodleBowlApp handles on X/Twitter, Instagram, Threads
- Set up Mailchimp or Loops for email list
- Draft App Store listing copy, screenshots, preview video concept

**Week 3–4: Press Pipeline**
- Identify 15–20 journalists and writers who cover: daily games, media literacy, mobile apps, productivity
  - Target beats: games/apps at The Verge, Wired, Fast Company; tech writers at NYT, WSJ; games/puzzle writers at any publication covering Wordle
  - Secondary: journalists and writers on Substack who cover games or media
- Draft a 150-word pitch email emphasizing the news-literacy angle ("a game that makes media literacy fun")
- Do not pitch yet — hold for timing around the Product Hunt launch

**Week 5–6: Content Bank Sprint**
- Fill question banks to launch minimum (30/30/20 — see Content Strategy in business plan)
- This is the critical path; everything else can slide but this cannot

**Week 7–8: Beta Seeding**
- Recruit 50–200 beta testers: journalists, podcast community members, friends-of-friends
- Goal: generate the first organic social shares and surface any UX friction before public launch
- Monitor challenge/help link usage rate in beta — if <5% are tapping, revisit copy before launch

### 6.2 Soft Launch

**Trigger:** 30+ questions per game, auth + content-from-Firestore shipped, smoke tests passing.

**Day 0:**
- Remove beta restriction from App Store listing (or submit as public release)
- Send launch email to waitlist
- Post across owned social channels
- Drop into relevant communities: r/wordle, r/dailygames, podcast Discord servers, Substack comment sections where games are discussed

**Day 0–7:** Watch retention curve. If Day-7 retention is above 30%, move to growth push. If below 20%, pause and investigate — likely a content quality or onboarding issue, not a distribution issue.

### 6.3 Growth Push

**Trigger:** 7-day retention >30% confirmed over a 2-week window.

- Lift press embargo — email the 15–20 journalists with a short briefing and early access
- Submit to Product Hunt (coordinate launch day with a supporter for upvotes)
- Brief 3–5 podcast/Substack creators for organic mentions (not paid; genuine seeding)
- Evaluate Apple Search Ads for head terms ("daily game," "brain game," "news quiz")

## 7. Viral Growth Loop

The in-product viral loop is the most important marketing mechanic. No paid campaign will match the conversion quality of a warm challenge from a friend.

### 7.1 The Two Loops

**Loop A — Challenge Loop (post-answer)**
```
Player finishes a game → taps "Challenge a Friend"
→ predicts friend's answer → shares link
→ friend plays on web (no install required)
→ sees comparison screen at peak engagement
→ sees App Store CTA → installs → plays daily → challenges their friends
```
Expected conversion: 15–25% of web players install (comparable to Wordle's early spread).

**Loop B — Help Loop (pre-answer)**
```
Player is stuck → taps "Stuck? Ask a Friend"
→ friend receives question link, answers it
→ both earn a streak shield (durable reward)
→ friend, having played the question, is engaged enough to install
```
Expected conversion: lower than challenge loop, but helper intent is higher — they already engaged with the question.

### 7.2 Optimizing the Loops

The post-challenge web page is the highest-leverage marketing asset in the product. It needs:
- Visual identity that immediately signals quality (Fraunces, paper aesthetic, no generic mobile look)
- The comparison reveal as the emotional hook ("You picked Dex. Ric picked Pip. Pip was right.")
- Download CTA that appears only after the comparison — not before, not during
- Copy that sells the daily habit, not just the single game: "Play fresh games every day →"

The challenge prediction step ("who do you think they'll pick?") also matters for shareability — it's what turns a share into a bet, and a bet is more compelling than a share.

### 7.3 Share Copy

Wordle-style result grids shared to group chats should include:
- Issue number (edition feel: "No. 0115")
- Game name
- Score/streak
- Emoji grid (green/red blocks, no answer spoilers)
- App URL or handle

Example:
```
Noodle Bowl No. 0115
The Lede · ✅ +10
The Spread · ❌ 
Science or Fiction · ✅✅✅ +50

🔥 14-day streak
noodlebowl.app
```

The result copy should be tunable — test whether including the streak or hiding it drives more shares.

## 8. App Store Optimization (ASO)

### 8.1 iOS App Store

**App Name:** Noodle Bowl — Daily Brain Games

**Subtitle (30 chars max):** Play the News Every Day

**Keywords (100 chars, comma-separated, no spaces):**
`daily game,brain game,news quiz,word puzzle,trivia,science game,headline game,connections,wordle`

**Promotional Text (170 chars — updateable without new release):**
"Three games. Five minutes. Real stories. Stretch your noodle every day with news-based games that reward curiosity over memory."

**Description (4000 chars):** Lead with the hook paragraph. Follow with the three game descriptions. End with the social mechanic and streak system. No bullet lists — editorial prose matches the brand.

**Screenshot strategy:**
- Screenshot 1: Hub screen — "Three games. One daily session."
- Screenshot 2: The Lede in play — reporter cards visible, bluffing mechanic clear
- Screenshot 3: The Spread reveal — "You were 8% off"
- Screenshot 4: Science or Fiction — claim cards with correct/incorrect revealed
- Screenshot 5: Challenge comparison — "You vs. Alex" side-by-side
- Screenshot 6: Streak + shields — the retention hook

**Preview video (30s):** Hub → play The Lede → reveal → challenge → friend playing on web → comparison screen. No voiceover. Music: upbeat, editorial, not gaming-y.

### 8.2 Google Play Store

Similar structure. Additional fields: short description (80 chars): "Daily news-based brain games. Challenge friends."

### 8.3 Ratings Strategy

Do not prompt for a review until the user has played for 5+ consecutive days. At that point, prompt during a high-satisfaction moment (after a perfect Science or Fiction round, or when a challenge comes back in their favor). Target 4.5+ average — essential for App Store editorial consideration.

## 9. Content Marketing & Social

### 9.1 Owned Social Channels

**Priority channel: X/Twitter.** The target audience (news-curious 25–45) is active on X, and the Wordle share format originated there. Daily result sharing will likely happen on X first.

**Secondary: Threads, Instagram.** Instagram Stories for visual game previews. Threads for the same audience who migrated from Twitter. LinkedIn for the "media literacy is important" professional angle.

**TikTok:** Lower priority pre-launch. If a creator makes a viral NB video organically, amplify. Don't force TikTok content for an audience that isn't there yet.

### 9.2 Social Content Calendar

**Daily (automated):** Post today's issue number and a non-spoiler tease of one game's topic. "No. 0115 — Today's Lede: something happened with scientists and octopuses. Finish the headline."

**3x per week (editorial):**
- Behind-the-scenes on how a question is constructed (builds trust in content quality)
- "Did you know?" style facts from The Spread reveals (educational + shareable)
- Aggregate result data: "82% of players thought it was satire. 73% were wrong." (social proof + curiosity)

**Weekly:**
- Streak leaderboard screenshot (anonymized or opted-in users)
- A Lede question "making of" — show the three reporter pitches before revealing which was real

**As needed:**
- Engage reply threads on any organic result shares
- Repost compelling result grids from players (with permission)

### 9.3 SEO / Web Presence

The web challenge page (`noodlebowl.app/c/{token}`) is the highest-traffic web page and it changes daily. The landing page needs to be indexed well.

Target long-tail search terms:
- "daily brain game like wordle"
- "news quiz game app"
- "science or fiction game"
- "daily headline game"
- "games to play with friends on your phone"

The editorial blog (if built) can target these terms with articles like "Why We Built a Brain Game Around Real News" or "The Psychology of Streak Mechanics."

## 10. Press & PR Strategy

### 10.1 Story Angles

The press pitch is not "new game app." The press pitches are:

**Angle 1 — Media Literacy as Play**
"A game that teaches people to spot satire and evaluate scientific claims — without feeling like a lesson. In an era of misinformation, this is what a media literacy tool looks like when it's fun."
→ Target: education, tech-culture writers. Fast Company, Wired, The Atlantic, NPR.

**Angle 2 — The Wordle Successor Story**
"After Wordle, what comes next? This app says: variety. Three games, one streak, all built from today's news."
→ Target: gaming and tech writers. The Verge, Ars Technica, Polygon adjacent beat.

**Angle 3 — The Social Mechanic**
"Instead of a group chat for sharing scores, this app lets you challenge a friend to the exact question you just played — and predict how they'll answer."
→ Target: social apps and mobile game writers. Business Insider, Mashable.

**Angle 4 — Solo Founder Building in Public**
Authentic story of building a polished, editorially considered game app as a solo project. Resonates with indie dev and Substack/maker communities.
→ Target: Indie Hackers, Product Hunt, Substack technology writers.

### 10.2 Target Publications and Writers

**Tier 1 (highest reach + alignment):**
- The Verge — mobile apps and games desk
- Wired — technology and culture
- Fast Company — productivity and apps
- NPR — media literacy, games that are also educational
- NYT — games/puzzle coverage (The Upshot, Tech section)

**Tier 2:**
- Ars Technica — technically literate audience
- The Information — tech apps
- Protocol / The Markup — digital media angle
- Lifehacker / The Strategist — practical "apps I use" format

**Tier 3 (high reach, lower conversion):**
- TechCrunch — app launches
- Mashable — lifestyle tech
- Business Insider — social apps

**Substack / newsletters:**
- Writers covering daily games (several Substack writers cover the NYT Games space)
- Media industry newsletters (Nieman Lab has covered games-as-journalism adjacent content)

### 10.3 Pitch Timing

- Reach out 2 weeks before soft launch with a personalized email + TestFlight/Play beta link
- No press embargo during beta — framing is "early access, would love your feedback"
- Growth push = formal embargo lift with press kit, screenshots, and a quote from the founder
- Do not pitch until the content bank is full — a journalist who plays 3 rounds before hitting a repeat question will not write a kind review

### 10.4 Press Kit Contents

- App icon (1024×1024), lockup variations
- 6 App Store screenshots (also usable in articles)
- 30-second preview video
- Founder bio (2 sentences)
- 3 game description paragraphs (one per game)
- Social loop description (challenge/help mechanic)
- Key metrics if available (beta user count, retention, challenge send rate)
- Boilerplate: "Noodle Bowl is a daily brain game app built around real news. Available on iOS and Android."

## 11. Partnership Opportunities

### 11.1 Media Partners

**The Skeptics' Guide to the Universe (SGU):** The Science or Fiction mechanic is directly modeled after their podcast segment. An official partnership ("powered by SGU content" or a co-branded Science or Fiction week) would be a natural fit and reach a loyal, highly relevant audience. Approach: reach out to the SGU team with a comp account and explain the inspiration.

**Wait Wait Don't Tell Me (NPR):** The Lede's reporter-persona bluffing mechanic has the same soul as Wait Wait's bluffing round. NPR is unlikely to officially partner (bureaucracy), but an individual mention from a Wait Wait panelist on social could be extremely high-value. Approach: organic — make a great product, find an in.

**Local and independent news organizations:** Many local news outlets are looking for engagement tools. A co-branded "City Edition" using local news stories could be a partnership model that helps both sides. Approach: propose a pilot to 2–3 outlets with active digital audiences.

### 11.2 Creator Partnerships

Target Substack and podcast creators who:
- Write about daily games (several Substack writers specifically cover NYT Games)
- Cover media literacy, misinformation, or news criticism
- Play and share Wordle/Connections in their content

Approach: gifted accounts, early access, no ask — let them play it and write about it naturally. A brief like "I think your readers would enjoy this, no ask for anything" outperforms a paid post disclosure for this audience.

### 11.3 App Store Editorial

Apple features apps in "New Games We Love" and similar collections. Editorial consideration requires:
- Clean, polished UI (NB has this)
- Timely hook (e.g., submit during a major news moment where the content is particularly relevant)
- Pre-submission contact with the App Store editorial team (submit a request via the developer portal 6 weeks before target feature date)

A feature in "New Games We Love" can drive 10k–50k downloads in a week. Worth the effort.

## 12. Email & Push

### 12.1 Email

**Pre-launch:** Waitlist email — weekly update on development, 1 game mechanic teased per email, "you'll be first to play" positioning.

**Post-launch welcome email:** Sent 24 hours after install (if email captured via account creation). Covers: how the streak works, the three games, the challenge/help mechanic. Warm, editorial tone. No bullet lists — prose.

**Weekly streak summary (optional, opt-in):** "Your week in Noodle Bowl" — streak status, best score of the week, a stat from that week's content ("73% of players thought it was real"). Doubles as a re-engagement email for lapsed players.

**Re-engagement (lapsed >7 days):** "Your streak is resting" — warm copy, no guilt. Single CTA: "Play today's edition."

### 12.2 Push Notifications

**Daily reminder (opt-in, 9 AM local time):** "Today's edition is ready. No. 0116 is out."

Keep it minimal and informative — not gamified ("Don't lose your streak!" creates anxiety, not motivation). The streak shield mechanic handles streak anxiety without the notification having to.

**Social notifications:**
- "Alex played your challenge — see how they did." (already implemented via Cloud Functions)
- "A hint came back from your friend." (help flow)

Do not send promotional push notifications. The social notifications are the only pushes that feel genuinely useful rather than interruptive.

**Avoid:** Meta/Instagram/TikTok paid until organic social proof exists. Paid creative for a game with no organic viral examples is expensive and low-converting.

## 14. Measurement & Analytics

### 14.1 Analytics Setup

**Firebase Analytics** (included, no extra cost): session tracking, event logging, funnel analysis. Configure custom events from day one:

| Event | When Fired |
|-------|-----------|
| `game_started` | Player taps Play on hub card |
| `game_completed` | Player reaches result screen |
| `challenge_sent` | Challenge link generated |
| `help_sent` | Help link generated |
| `challenge_web_opened` | Friend opens challenge on web |
| `challenge_web_completed` | Friend completes challenge on web |
| `app_installed_from_challenge` | First open attributed to challenge link (Branch) |
| `streak_shield_earned` | Shield awarded |
| `streak_broken` | Streak reset (no shield available) |
| `account_created` | Email/password sign-up completed |

### 14.2 Key Dashboards

**Daily health dashboard (check every day):**
- DAU, WAU, MAU
- DAU/MAU ratio
- Games played per DAU
- Streak break rate

**Weekly retention dashboard (check weekly):**
- D1, D7, D14, D30 retention by cohort
- Median streak length
- % users with 7+ day streak, 30+ day streak

**Social loop dashboard (check weekly):**
- Challenge send rate (% of completed game sessions)
- Challenge web open rate
- Web → app conversion rate
- Help send rate
- Shield earn rate

**Content quality dashboard (check per content drop):**
- Per-game completion rate (drop-off = confusion or boredom)
- Per-question accuracy rate (if skewing >85%, too easy; <30%, too hard)
- SoF Weird & True vs. standard completion parity

### 14.3 Attribution

Use Branch.io for link attribution. Configure:
- Challenge link → `campaign: challenge, channel: in-app`
- Help link → `campaign: help, channel: in-app`
- Social share (result grid post) → `campaign: organic_share, channel: social`
- Press coverage → `campaign: press, channel: editorial`

Track install-to-D7-retention by attribution source. Challenge-driven installs should retain better than press-driven installs (higher intent, social accountability).

### 14.4 Qualitative Signals

**App Store reviews:** Read every review for the first 3 months. Note which games are mentioned, which pain points recur, what delights users.

**Social listening:** Search `noodlebowl.app`, `#NoodleBowl`, and the game names weekly. Respond to shares. Build the perception that the maker is present.

**Beta user interviews:** Schedule 5–10 video calls with beta users before launch. Ask: "What did you expect when you first opened it?" "Which game do you look forward to most?" "What would make you stop playing?"
